01 — Manifesto

p. 002

A commission is not a request. It is a record.

Creative work is long. It is briefed on a Monday, argued over for a month, approved by someone who was away when the revision landed, and delivered after the third round. Most systems forget this between one step and the next. VELIN is built for it.

A durable workflow does not start over when a service does. It resumes from the last step that completed, waits as long as a decision takes, and treats an approval as a fact tied to one revision — not a click.

Every artifact leaves with its lineage: the brief that asked for it, the revision it answers, the person who accepted it, and the steps that failed along the way.

04 — Process

p. 046

Five movements.

01

Brief

The commission arrives with its constraints and its approvers named. It is read twice before anything runs.

02

Execute

Steps run in order and at length. A step that fails is retried; a step that waits is not a failure.

03

Review

A revision is presented as it is, with its lineage attached. Nothing is hidden behind a summary.

04

Approve

A named person accepts, rejects or asks for a change. The decision binds to that revision alone.

05

Continue

The workflow resumes from the decision — next step, next revision or delivery. The record grows; it never restarts.
